# Battle for La Roma — voice and style

Status: **approved by the owner** (2026-11-05), with the note: don't shy away from being snarky, or even a little tacky, when it's warranted. This applies to the landing page, store pages, in-game text and campaign writing, in Spanish (es-MX) and English.

## The voice in one line

**The neighborhood chronicler (*el cronista de la colonia*).** A chilango who has lived through every rent increase, narrates the war for La Roma like the neighborhood WhatsApp group crossed with a football commentator, and includes themselves in the joke.

It is affectionate with a bite: ironic, warm, specific, and never cruel to the people it describes. Snark is welcome, and so is a little tackiness when the moment earns it (a bad pun, a mock-solemn disclaimer). The line it does not cross is punching at people.

## Five principles

1. **Clear first, funny second.** Each block first says plainly what the player does (build, gather, advance an age, take La Roma), then adds the joke. This follows the agreed PMF rule that the economy, combat and path to La Roma make sense before any cultural reference does. A player who misses the joke still knows what the game is.
2. **Laugh at situations, prices and pretension, and at ourselves.** The targets are rent, landlords, traffic, coffee that comes with a biography, corporate jargon and our own past selves. The targets are never a person's origin, accent, class or appearance. Factions are rivals, like football clubs, and none of them is a caricature of real people. The self-inclusive move ("you said that. Out loud.") is the heart of the voice. It is also how Mexican internet writing on gentrification sounds: angry and in love with the city at the same time.
3. **Specific beats generic, but context must carry it.** Use real textures: a 5-gallon water jug (*garrafón*), changing metro lines at Centro Médico, a meeting that could have been an email. Write each detail so its meaning is clear from the sentence around it. If a reference needs a footnote, cut it.
4. **Short, punchy, in threes.** Short sentences. A rhythm of three. A dry aside that deflates the line before it ("Es una experiencia urbana curada." / "(Ajá.)").
5. **Each joke lands once.** Every recurring motif has a budget (see below). Repetition is what makes the current page feel soulless: seven rooftop rooms and five *aguas locas* turn a joke into wallpaper.

## Spanish (es-MX) rules

- **Mexican Spanish, spoken register.** Address the player as *tú*. Use *ustedes*, never *vosotros*. Avoid Spain-only words (*vale*, *coger*, *ordenador*, *móvil*). Prefer *celular* and *computadora*.
- **Slang sparingly and correctly.** *Chido*, *neta*, *no manches*, *qué oso* are fine at most once per page, and only where a local would say them. No profanity on public pages. *Pinche* and stronger words stay out of marketing.
- **Accents on capitals.** GENTRIFICACIÓN, ÚLTIMA HORA, CAMPAÑA. Uppercase never drops the accent.
- **Prefer the Mexican spelling when the RAE accepts two.** *coctel / cocteles* (not *cóctel*), *video* (not *vídeo*), *chofer* (not *chófer*).
- **"solo" without an accent**, following RAE since 2010 (the current page has *sólo*).
- **One quotation style.** Use « » for quoted dialogue throughout. The current page mixes “ ” and « ».
- **Times:** *8:00 a. m.*, or *8:00 A. M.* in uppercase stamps. **Money:** *$95*, *$1,800/mes*. **City:** *CDMX* or *Ciudad de México*, never *México D.F.*
- **Inclusive by construction, not by symbols.** Write *Te damos la bienvenida* instead of *Bienvenido*, and *quien juega* instead of *el jugador*. Do not use *-e* or *-x* endings in game copy.
- **Fixed terms** (see glossary): *godín* (singular and adjective) and *godínez* (plural, colloquial); *mirrey* in lowercase as a common noun; *foráneo* or *foránea*; game resources capitalized: *Comida, Materiales, Varos, Terrenos*.

## English rules

- **Write it fresh (transcreation), don't translate.** The English voice is a local friend showing you around: dry, wry and a little proud. It keeps Spanish proper nouns (La Roma, Godín Age, Varos) and explains them through context the first time: "the Godín Age — badge, cubicle, Teams status set to *busy*."
- Jokes may change between languages, but each **section's claim stays identical**, because features and facts must match.
- US spelling, sentence case for body text, ALL CAPS only for display headlines.
- No mock-Spanish ("el cheapo", "no problemo") and no accent jokes.
