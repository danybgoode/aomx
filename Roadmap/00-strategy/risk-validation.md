# Risk Validation Plan — Battle for La Roma

## Broad validation

| Dimension | Analogs (success to mimic) | Antilogs (failure to avoid) |
|---|---|---|
| Problem to solve | Classic RTS resource, economy, and combat loops that players already understand. (your words) | A premise that is funny to describe but weak to play. (assumed) |
| Target audience | Players familiar with Age of Empires II and Mexican gaming culture. (your words) | A game that only makes sense to people who know the references. (your words) |
| Value proposition | A familiar RTS structure in a distinctive Mexico City setting. (your words) | Cultural jokes obscuring objectives or strategy. (your words) |

## Highest domino

**Dimension:** Value proposition (assumed)

**Hypothesis:** Classic RTS players will want another match after trying the student-to-neighborhood conquest loop, and players unfamiliar with Mexican references will still understand how to win. (assumed)

## Targeted technique

Prototypes. (assumed)

## Execution rationale

Build a small playable encounter with one economy loop, a district objective, and two meaningfully different regional factions; test it with both culturally familiar and unfamiliar RTS players. (assumed)
Instrument first open, match start, match completion, and later-day completion before inviting testers; reconcile the first test players against raw events so the launch funnel is trustworthy from its first cohort. (assumed)
Ask each player to explain the objective and next strategic choice without help, then offer an unprompted second match; success means both groups can explain the rules and a meaningful share choose to play again. (assumed)
Set a numeric threshold and sample size before running the test so the outcome cannot be rationalized afterward. (assumed)
